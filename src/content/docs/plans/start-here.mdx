## The situation

You're about to spend money on an AI subscription and you want a straight answer, not a
comparison chart. This page gives you one, plus the reasoning behind it, so you can tell
whether it fits your situation or not. (Exact prices are on each plan's own page — they
change more often than this recommendation does.)

## Prerequisites

None. Just an honest sense of what you'll mostly be doing with it — writing code daily, or
something lighter.

## Steps

1. **Ask what you're mostly doing.** If it's daily coding — especially agentic coding,
   where you hand off a task and let the tool work — that points one direction clearly.
   If it's general writing, research, and occasional code, both options work about
   equally well.

2. **Ask how you feel about being interrupted vs. quietly downgraded.** This is the
   question most people skip and regret skipping. See "How the two failure modes feel"
   below before you answer it.

3. **Ask what your budget actually is.** One entry-tier plan, or roughly double that if
   you'd rather not choose.

## Decision points

**Pick [Claude Pro](../what-you-get/claude-pro/) if** you're doing daily agentic
coding and can live with a hard stop at your session limit rather than a degraded
experience.

**Pick [ChatGPT Plus](../what-you-get/chatgpt-plus/) if** you'd rather have continuity
— getting bumped to a lighter model under load — over being blocked outright, or if Codex
alongside general chat use fits your workflow better.

**Pick both if** your budget allows roughly double an entry-tier plan and you don't want
to choose — a genuinely common outcome, not a cop-out. Plenty of people run Claude Code
for complex agentic work and keep a second subscription for everyday chat.

### How the two failure modes feel

This is the single most useful thing to know before you buy, and it's not what most
people assume. Claude's paid plans hit a hard wall at the session boundary: the tool
simply stops responding until the window resets, unless you've opted into pay-as-you-go
overflow. ChatGPT's plans are reported to degrade rather than block outright — you're more
likely to get a weaker model than a flat refusal (this detail is community-reported for
ChatGPT, not independently confirmed — see its plan page). Decide which experience you'd
rather have mid-task, not after you've hit it for the first time.

### What free tiers can actually do

Both [Claude Free](../what-you-get/claude-free/) and
[ChatGPT Free](../what-you-get/chatgpt-free/) are real products worth trying before
you spend anything — they just won't support daily coding work. Start there if you're not
sure you'll use either one regularly.

## What good looks like

You've picked a plan based on how its limit actually behaves, not its marketing page, and
you know in advance what hitting that limit will feel like.

## What goes wrong

Buying based on feature lists instead of failure modes, then being surprised the first
time a long session gets cut off. Or assuming a free tier will cover real daily work
because the paid price felt unnecessary until it wasn't.
