## At a glance

<ModelFacts modelId="claude-fable-5" />

## Best at

Long-running autonomous agent work — tasks where the model needs to keep working across
many steps without losing the thread. Anthropic itself calls this its most capable widely
released model.

## Weak at

Noticeably slower than Sonnet or Haiku, which matters for anything interactive. It also
has no explicit extended-thinking toggle — its reasoning is always-on and automatic, not
something you turn on for a specific hard problem and off for a quick one.

## Quota weight

This is the heaviest model on Claude Pro or Max. Reach for it for a genuinely hard,
high-stakes step — architecture decisions, a gnarly multi-file refactor, the kind of task
where getting it right the first time matters more than getting it fast. It's a poor
default for routine cleanup or quick questions, where its cost buys you nothing extra.

## When to pick it

When the task is long-running, ambiguous, or high-stakes enough that you'd rather spend
more quota than redo the work.

## When not to

For anything you'd be comfortable delegating to a faster, cheaper sibling — most everyday
coding falls into this category. Don't reach for Fable 5 just because it's the newest or
most capable option available.

## Notes and gotchas

Its reasoning is always-on, so you can't dial it down for a simple sub-task the way you
can toggle extended thinking off on Haiku. If a session mixes easy and hard steps,
consider switching models between them rather than running the whole thing on Fable 5.
