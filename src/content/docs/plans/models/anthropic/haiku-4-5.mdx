## Best at

Speed and cost. It's the fastest Claude model, the cheapest per token, and — unlike the
current Claude 5 family — has an explicit extended-thinking toggle you can turn on for a
specific hard step and leave off otherwise.

## Weak at

Its context window is meaningfully smaller than the other current Claude models' (see the
figures above), which matters for very large codebases or documents. It's also not
positioned for the most complex agentic coding.

## Quota weight

Light. This is the model to leave running for routine work, quick questions, and anything
where speed matters more than maximum capability.

## When to pick it

Fast iteration, simple tasks, high-volume or repetitive work, and anywhere cost matters
more than squeezing out the last bit of capability.

## When not to

Large-context work that exceeds 200k tokens, or genuinely complex agentic coding that
benefits from a heavier sibling.

## Notes and gotchas

Unlike the Claude 5 family, Haiku 4.5's reasoning is opt-in rather than always-on — turn
on extended thinking explicitly for a hard sub-task, and leave it off for everything else
to keep it fast.
