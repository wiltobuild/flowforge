## Who this is for

Someone deciding whether Claude fits their work at all, before spending anything. Good
for a first look — chatting, trying a small piece of writing or code, seeing whether
Claude's style suits you.

## What you get

<PlanFacts planId="claude-free" />

## What it's good at

Occasional, light use: a question here, a short draft there, trying a feature before
paying for it. It's a real product, not a crippled demo — you get the full chat interface
and a genuine set of tools, just with a small window to use them in.

## What it can't do

It isn't built for daily work. There's no Claude Code on Free, so it's not a coding
assistant in the way Pro or Max are. And because there's no way to pay for more room when
the session limit hits, a single ambitious task can burn the whole window in one sitting.

## How the limits feel

You'll notice the free tier when you're in the middle of something and the response
stops coming. There's no in-between state — no warning that you're close, no option to
push through. You wait out the reset or you don't finish today. That's the deliberate
trade for $0: the plan intentionally leaves no lever to pull when you hit the wall.

## Who should pick something else

Anyone who wants to write or edit code regularly should look at [Claude
Pro](../claude-pro/) instead — Free doesn't include Claude Code at all.
Anyone who's hit the Free session limit more than once or twice already knows the answer.
